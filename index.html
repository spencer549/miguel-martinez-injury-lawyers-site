Can I file a personal injury claim if I’m undocumented?

Yes. In Colorado, your immigration status does not affect your legal right to file a personal injury or workers’ compensation claim. You may seek compensation for your injuries regardless of your documentation, and we use specific legal strategies aimed at keeping your status out of the courtroom.

How much does it cost to hire a personal injury attorney?

Nothing upfront. We work on a contingency fee basis, so we only get paid if we win your case. Colorado contingency fees typically range from 30–40% of the final recovery, and we discuss this clearly before you sign anything. If we don’t win, you owe us nothing.

How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Colorado?

For most personal injury cases, Colorado law gives you 2 years from the date of injury. Car accident cases have a 3-year window under Colorado’s statute of limitations (C.R.S. § 13-80-101). Do not wait. Evidence disappears and witnesses’ memories fade. Call us today.

Will filing a lawsuit put my immigration status at risk?

This is the question we get most often. Defense attorneys sometimes try to introduce immigration status to intimidate plaintiffs into settling for less. We know this tactic and we work to block it. Your status should not be admissible in a personal injury case, and we fight to keep it that way.

What if I was partially at fault for the accident?

Colorado follows a “Modified Comparative Negligence” rule under C.R.S. § 13-21-111. If you were less than 50% at fault, you can still recover compensation. It’s just reduced by your percentage of fault. Being partly to blame does not automatically end a claim.

What happens to my medical bills while my case is pending?

We negotiate directly with Denver Health, Saint Joseph Hospital, and other local providers to seek reductions in hospital liens. A reduced lien can mean more of a settlement stays with you instead of going straight to medical debt.

How long will my case take?

Many straightforward injury cases in Colorado resolve within 6–18 months. Complex cases involving serious injuries, commercial vehicles, or disputed liability can take longer. We give you a realistic timeline at your consultation, not a vague non-answer.
